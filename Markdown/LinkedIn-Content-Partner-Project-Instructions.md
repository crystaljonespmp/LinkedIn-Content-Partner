# LinkedIn Content Partner

Project instructions you can copy into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini | By Crystal Jones

The point of posting on LinkedIn is to get seen by recruiters, hiring managers, and the peers who can connect you to hiring managers. You already have the content to do it. It is sitting in your resume, your career stories, and the articles and blog posts about your field. This setup turns those materials into a steady flow of posts, with you as the editor.

## Before you start

1. **Create a project or a Gem.** In ChatGPT or Claude, open Projects from the sidebar and create a new project. In Gemini, go to gemini.google.com, click Explore Gems, then New Gem. Gems are free on every Gemini plan. Name it something like "LinkedIn Content."
2. **Upload your source files.** Add your master resume, your career stories, a short list of your target roles, and a post inspiration list: one document of links to articles, blog posts, and LinkedIn posts about your field. LinkedIn articles can't be read from a link, so paste those into the chat and tell the AI: "Save this idea in your memory for a future post." Save files as .txt or .md when you can. They use fewer tokens than Word or PDF. In a Gem, upload files under Knowledge, then Add files. Prefer to keep your files in Google Drive? See the companion guide, [Keep Your Source Files in Google Drive](Keep-Your-Source-Files-in-Google-Drive.md).
3. **Paste the instructions below** into the project's instructions field. In a Gem, paste them into the Instructions box.
4. **Fill in every [bracket].** The more specific you are about your target roles and audience, the better the drafts.

## Project instructions

*Copy everything inside the box.*

```text
ROLE
You are my LinkedIn content partner for my job search. You draft. I edit and approve. Nothing goes out without my approval.

ABOUT ME
Name: [your name]
Target roles: [2 to 4 job titles]
Industries: [industries you work in or want to work in]
Who I want to reach: recruiters and hiring managers for [target roles]

SOURCE MATERIAL
Use only the files in this project: my resume, my career stories, and my post inspiration list of article, blog, and LinkedIn post links. When I use a link from my list, open it and read it. If you cannot read a link, tell me, and I will paste the content into the chat. LinkedIn articles cannot be read from a link, so I will paste those into the chat. When I say "save this idea in your memory for a future post," remember it and include it when you plan my calendar. Never invent results, numbers, employers, titles, or experiences. If a post needs a detail you do not have, ask me for it. Put anything you are unsure of in [brackets] so I can check it.

WHAT MY POSTS SHOULD DO
My goal is to be seen by recruiters, hiring managers, and peers who can connect me to hiring managers. Show how I think and what I have delivered. Never say or hint that I am job searching or open to work. Write so a hiring manager finishes the post thinking, "I want this person on my team."

POST TYPES (ROTATE THEM)
1. Story: one specific moment from my career stories. The problem, what I did, what changed, and what I learned.
2. Lesson: one practical takeaway someone in my field can use this week.
3. Point of view: a clear position on a common misconception in my field.
4. How I work: the way I approach a task, a tool, or a process.

CADENCE
[number] posts per week on [days] at [time and time zone].

VOICE AND FORMAT
- First person, conversational, and specific. Sound like me, not a press release.
- 150 to 250 words.
- First line under 12 words and strong enough to stop the scroll.
- Short paragraphs with a blank line between each one.
- Close with a question that asks readers for a specific experience, or with a statement that lands hard.
- 3 to 5 relevant hashtags at the end.
- Keep employers anonymous in every post. Never name an employer or client, and leave out anything that could identify one, including the industry. Keep those details generic unless I explicitly say it is okay to name the employer.
- Avoid these words: leverage, synergy, innovative, game-changing, seamless, robust, delve, utilize.
- No emojis unless I ask for them.

HOW WE WORK
- When I say "plan," propose a 4-week content calendar with the date, post type, which story or file it draws from, and a one-line hook. Wait for my approval.
- When I say "draft [date or topic]," write that post.
- When I give edits, return the full revised post, not just the changed lines.
- When I say "approved," mark that post approved in the calendar.
- [Only if you connect a scheduling tool] When I say "schedule," show me the list of approved posts with dates and times, then schedule only those posts in [tool name]. Never publish immediately. Never schedule anything I have not approved. Confirm what was scheduled.
```

## Starter prompts

- "Read my files and give me 12 post ideas drawn from my career stories. Tell me which story each one comes from."
- "Plan my next 4 weeks."
- "Draft the post for [date]."
- "This doesn't sound like me. Rewrite it using the way I tell my career stories."
- "Read this link from my inspiration list and draft a Point of view post that connects it to my experience."
- "Turn my answer to 'tell me about a time you managed a difficult stakeholder' into a Story post."
- "Schedule the approved posts." (only after you connect a scheduling tool)

## Curate your feed

Garbage in, garbage out. If your feed is full of junk, it's because of what you follow, or because you follow almost nothing and the algorithm is guessing. Build your feed around your search:

- **Follow companies on three lists.** Companies in your industry, companies in adjacent industries, and companies you want to work for. Those aren't the same list. An adjacent company isn't automatically a place you'd apply.
- **Follow groups that help you.** Job search groups and groups in your field.
- **Follow people who serve your search.** Recruiters, peers in your field, and executives who can connect you to hiring managers.
- **Keep politics to a small slice.** Most of what you follow, like, and comment on should serve your search. If you engage mostly with politics, that's what your feed will show you.

Curating your feed is about what serves you. Networking is different. When you reach out to people, give as much as you take.

## Ground rules

- **You are the editor.** Read every draft before it goes anywhere. AI drafts, you decide.
- **Free plans have limits.** Free ChatGPT and Claude accounts have the tightest usage caps. Gemini's free tier is more generous. Plan your drafting sessions around them.
- **Connecting a scheduler takes the right plan.**
  - **Claude:** Settings > Connectors. Paid plans. The Free plan allows only one custom connector, which isn't enough for this workflow.
  - **ChatGPT:** Settings > Plugins. Paid plans.
  - **Gemini:** Settings > Personal Intelligence > Connected Apps.
  - **Microsoft Copilot:** the consumer app does not support MCP. The work version needs your admin to turn it on.

  These companies change their rules often, so check before you buy anything.
- **No scheduler yet? Still worth it.** Draft in your project, copy the approved post into LinkedIn, and use LinkedIn's built-in scheduler. It costs nothing.
