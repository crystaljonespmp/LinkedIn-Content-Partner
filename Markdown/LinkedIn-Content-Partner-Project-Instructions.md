# LinkedIn Content Partner

Part 1: Setup | Project instructions you can copy into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini | By Crystal Jones

The point of posting on LinkedIn is to get seen by recruiters, hiring managers, and the peers who can connect you to hiring managers. You already have the content to do it. It is sitting in your resume, your career stories, and the articles and blog posts about your field. This setup turns those materials into a steady flow of posts, with you as the editor. This is the foundation of your networking strategy: the work that makes people recognize you before you ever reach out.

## This is your networking strategy, not social media

If you think of LinkedIn the way you think of Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok, you're missing the point. LinkedIn is built for professional networking, and it's the social platform for job seekers. Job seekers don't go to Facebook for this kind of engagement. LinkedIn isn't for telling people what you had for lunch. X has become a place where job seekers network too, even though it's traditional social media.

Most people think networking means reaching out to someone who might help them land a job. That's the last step, not the first. Networking starts with engagement: posting what you know, commenting on other people's work, and showing up consistently where hiring managers, recruiters, and your peers already spend their time. Every post you publish and every comment you leave builds your professional reputation. By the time you reach out to someone, they've already seen how you think.

### LinkedIn's algorithm rewards this

LinkedIn now ranks posts with an AI model called 360Brew. LinkedIn's own research describes it reading your profile, your activity, and the text of posts to decide who sees what. ([LinkedIn research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16450)) LinkedIn has said it no longer rewards posts designed to game the system the way it used to, and that it wants to surface knowledge and advice from people with real expertise. ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-shares-new-insights-into-its-latest-algorithm-updates/654091/)) Analysts who track LinkedIn reach report that posts matching your profile's expertise travel further, while engagement bait and viral tricks travel less. ([Top Dog Social Media](https://topdogsocialmedia.com/?p=548196)) That's what this guide has you doing: posting from your real experience, in your field, consistently.

### Give as well as take

Networking works when you give as well as take. People notice who only shows up when they need something. Being engaged while you are searching shows you're not that person. Staying engaged after you're hired is how you show you're not that person.

### After you land the job

Don't stop. Once you've built that reputation, letting it go quiet means starting over if you're ever back in the job market. Keep posting, connecting, and engaging. You can turn the temperature down, from three posts a week to one, or keep the same pace. I'm keeping mine.

## Before you start

1. **Create a project or a Gem.** In ChatGPT or Claude, open Projects from the sidebar and create a new project. In Gemini, go to gemini.google.com, click Explore Gems, then New Gem. Gems are free on every Gemini plan. Name it something like "LinkedIn Content."
2. **Upload your source files.** Add your master resume, your career stories, a short list of your target roles, and a post inspiration list: one document of articles, blog posts, and LinkedIn posts about your field. See "Your post inspiration list" below for what to save for each one. Save files as .txt or .md when you can. They use fewer tokens than Word or PDF. In a Gem, upload files under Knowledge, then Add files. Prefer to keep your files in Google Drive? See the companion guide, [Keep Your Source Files in Google Drive](Keep-Your-Source-Files-in-Google-Drive.md).
3. **Paste the instructions below** into the project's instructions field. In a Gem, paste them into the Instructions box.
4. **Fill in every [bracket].** The more specific you are about your target roles and audience, the better the drafts. One bracket asks whether your posts can mention that you're job searching. That's your call.
5. **Start one chat per month.** Each month, open a new chat in your project. Start it with your monthly review, then plan the month and draft that month's posts in the same chat. Your instructions and files carry into every chat. The month's calendar and drafts live in that month's chat.

## Your post inspiration list

Your inspiration list is one running document of things you've read that are worth reacting to. Add to it whenever you find something. Over time, it becomes a library of post ideas. What you save depends on where it came from:

- **Articles and blog posts on the open web:** save the link. Your AI can open and read it.
- **Articles behind a paywall:** save the full text and the link. Your AI can't get past a paywall.
- **LinkedIn posts:** save the full text of the post and the link. Your AI can't read LinkedIn from a link.
- **LinkedIn articles:** save the full text of the article and the link, for the same reason.

When you find something mid-conversation, you can also paste it into the chat and say "Save this idea in your memory for a future post." Add it to your inspiration list too. Memory varies by AI tool and plan, but your list is there every month.

## Choosing your cadence

These recommendations come from published research, not guesswork.

**Start with two or three posts a week.** Buffer analyzed more than 2 million LinkedIn posts and found that posting 2 to 5 times a week grows your reach sustainably. Its researchers say plainly that one post a week isn't enough. ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/))

**Pick your days and times to start.** Sprout Social's 2026 data, drawn from about 2 billion engagements, shows Tuesday through Thursday, late morning through afternoon, as the most consistent window. That's in your audience's time zone, not yours. Weekends are the weakest. ([Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-linkedin/))

**Then let your own numbers decide.** Averages are a starting point, not your answer. An analysis of nearly 8,000 posts from 63 LinkedIn profiles found that the best day to post was different for 80% of people. It also found that you need at least three posts on a given day before that day's results mean anything. ([Trevor Fox](https://trevorfox.com/2026/06/the-best-day-to-post-on-linkedin-is-different-for-everyone/))

**Give it time.** Stick with your cadence for two or three months so your monthly review has enough posts to show you what works for you.

## How often you do this

**Pick your batch size.** Plan and draft a week, two weeks, or a month at a time. It depends on you, and it can change month to month. If you already have ideas tied to specific dates, draft and schedule those first, then build out the rest.

**Pick one day and stick to it.** Batching on the same day every week or every two weeks is what keeps this from taking over your week.

**Run the monthly review on the first weekend after the month ends.** If the first day or two of the new month fall on your posting days, make sure those posts are ready before the month ends. Then run the review, plan the month, and draft.

**How long it takes.** In my experience, at three posts a week, drafting and posting two weeks of content by hand takes about an hour and a half to two hours.

## Personal posts

We're human, and the occasional personal post belongs on LinkedIn. The difference from Facebook or Instagram is that it's done thoughtfully. Share the moment as a person first, then let one line show how you think as a professional. Celebrating my mother-in-law's birthday over lunch is the personal moment. The project manager in me making sure the reservation was set and every detail was handled is the professional thread. Keep the human side front and center. Leave the rest for Facebook.

## Project instructions

*Copy everything inside the box.*

```text
ROLE
You are my LinkedIn content partner. You draft posts, and you review posts I write. I edit and approve. Nothing goes out without my approval.

ABOUT ME
Name: [your name]
Target roles: [2 to 4 job titles]
Industries: [industries you work in or want to work in]
Who I want to reach: recruiters and hiring managers for [target roles]

SOURCE MATERIAL
Use only the files in this project: my resume, my career stories, and my post inspiration list. My inspiration list has links to articles and blog posts, plus the full text of LinkedIn posts, LinkedIn articles, and paywalled articles, each with its link. When an entry is only a link, open it and read it. If you cannot read a link, tell me, and I will add the full text to my list. When I paste something into the chat and say "save this idea in your memory for a future post," remember it and include it when you plan my calendar. I will also add it to my inspiration list. Never invent results, numbers, employers, titles, or experiences. If a post needs a detail you do not have, ask me for it. Put anything you are unsure of in [brackets] so I can check it.

WHAT MY POSTS SHOULD DO
My goal is to be seen by recruiters, hiring managers, and peers who can connect me to hiring managers. Show me as a subject matter expert with clear opinions about my field. Every post should answer two questions. For a hiring manager: why should we hire this person? For a potential colleague: why would I want to work with this person? Show how I think and what I have delivered.
Job search: [choose one: keep my job search out of my posts / it is okay to say I am looking for my next role]

POST TYPES (ROTATE THEM)
1. Story: one specific moment from my career stories. The problem, what I did, what changed, and what I learned.
2. Lesson: one practical takeaway someone in my field can use this week.
3. Point of view: a clear position on a common misconception in my field.
4. How I work: the way I approach a task, a tool, or a process.
5. Personal (occasionally): a real moment from my life, told as a person first, with one line that shows how I think as a professional. Never a play-by-play of my day.

CADENCE
[number] posts per week on [days] at [time and time zone]. I plan and draft [one week / two weeks / one month] at a time.

VOICE AND FORMAT
- First person, conversational, and specific. Sound like me, not a press release.
- 150 to 250 words.
- First line under 12 words and strong enough to stop the scroll.
- Short paragraphs with a blank line between each one.
- Close with a question that asks readers for a specific experience, or with a statement that lands hard.
- 3 to 5 relevant hashtags at the end.
- Keep employers anonymous in every post. Never name an employer or client, and leave out anything that could identify one, including the industry. Keep those details generic unless I explicitly say it is okay to name the employer.
- Avoid these words: leverage, synergy, innovative, game-changing, seamless, robust, delve, utilize.
- No emojis unless I ask for them.

HOW WE WORK
- When I say "plan," propose a content calendar for my planning window, or for the dates I give you, with the date, post type, which story or file it draws from, and a one-line hook. Wait for my approval.
- When I say "draft [date or topic]," write that post.
- When I bring you a post I wrote, review it. Tighten it, clean up the grammar and flow, and flag anything that breaks these instructions. Keep my voice and my point. Return the full revised post and a short list of what you changed.
- When I give edits, return the full revised post, not just the changed lines.
- When I say "approved," mark that post approved in the calendar.

MONTHLY REVIEW
At the start of each month, I will start a new chat, give you my LinkedIn analytics file, and say "monthly review." Then:
- Match the posts in the file to our calendar, so you know each post's type, topic, and source.
- Tell me which post types, topics, opening lines, days, and times got the most impressions, comments, and new followers, and which fell flat.
- Tell me who is reading: job titles, industries, and seniority. Tell me whether I am reaching recruiters and hiring managers for my target roles.
- Compare this month to earlier months if I have given you those files. Call something a pattern only when the numbers support it. One month is a small sample, so say so.
- Recommend changes, ranked from most to least impact, with the numbers behind each one. Keep what is working. Label each one:
  - Plan change: it only affects next month's calendar, like more Story posts.
  - Instruction change: it updates a standing rule in my instructions, like my cadence, post types, audience, or voice.
- Do not change anything until I approve. Use approved plan changes the next time I say "plan."
- For approved instruction changes, work from my current project instructions word for word. If you cannot read them, tell me, and I will paste them into the chat. Then give me my full updated project instructions with the changes made, so I can replace my instructions in one paste, and list exactly what you changed. You cannot edit my instructions yourself, so remind me to paste them in.
```

## Starter prompts

- "Read my files and give me 12 post ideas drawn from my career stories. Tell me which story each one comes from."
- "Plan my next two weeks." or "Plan next month."
- "Draft the post for [date]."
- "This doesn't sound like me. Rewrite it using the way I tell my career stories."
- "Here's a post I wrote. Tighten it up and keep my voice."
- "Read this link from my inspiration list and draft a Point of view post that connects it to my experience."
- "Turn my answer to 'tell me about a time you managed a difficult stakeholder' into a Story post."
- "Here is my LinkedIn analytics file for [month]. Run my monthly review."

## Curate your feed

Garbage in, garbage out. If your feed is full of junk, it's because of what you follow, or because you follow almost nothing and the algorithm is guessing. Build your feed around your search:

- **Follow companies on three lists.** Companies in your industry, companies in adjacent industries, and companies you want to work for. Those aren't the same list. An adjacent company isn't automatically a place you'd apply.
- **Follow groups that help you.** Job search groups and groups in your field.
- **Follow people who serve your search.** Recruiters, peers in your field, and executives who can connect you to hiring managers.
- **Keep politics to a small slice.** Most of what you follow, like, and comment on should serve your search. If you engage mostly with politics, that's what your feed will show you.

Curating your feed is about what serves you. Networking is different. When you reach out to people, give as much as you take.

## Measure what's working, once a month

Posting without measuring is guessing. In the first week of each month, pull your LinkedIn analytics, give them to your AI, and let the numbers shape next month's plan.

### Pull your analytics

1. **Open your analytics.** On a computer, go to your LinkedIn profile, find the **Analytics** section, and click **Post impressions**.
2. **Set the date range.** Choose **Past 28 days**. Custom date ranges are only in the LinkedIn mobile app.
3. **Click Export** in the top right. LinkedIn downloads an Excel file with your impressions, engagement, top posts, new followers, and the job titles, industries, and seniority of the people who saw your posts.
4. **Name the file with the month,** like "LinkedIn Analytics 2026-11," so you can compare months later.

To check a single post, open the post, click **View analytics**, then click **Export**.

### Give it to your AI

Add the file to your project so the AI can compare months over time. In a Gem, add it under Knowledge. Then start the month's new chat in your project, attach the file, and say: "Here is my LinkedIn analytics file for [month]. Run my monthly review."

### What to look for

- **Who's reading.** Job titles, industries, and seniority. You want recruiters and hiring managers for your target roles to see your posts, so this matters more than total impressions.
- **Comments.** A comment is a conversation, and conversations start relationships. A comment is worth more than a reaction.
- **New followers.** More of the right people choosing to see your posts.
- **What's winning.** Which post types, topics, opening lines, days, and times do best.

One month is a small sample. Look for patterns that hold for two or three months before you make big changes.

### How it changes your plan

The review ends with recommended changes, ranked from most to least impact. You approve the ones you want, then say "plan." For example:

- **Story posts start the most conversations.** Plan more Story posts.
- **Your readers aren't in your target industry.** Write more about the problems that industry cares about.
- **One posting day keeps beating the others.** Move a post to that day.

Keep what's working. You don't have to take every recommendation. Changing a few things at a time makes next month's results easier to read, because you can tell which change made the difference.

### When your instructions need to change

Some findings change more than next month's calendar. If your Tuesday posts keep winning, your cadence should say Tuesday. If recruiters in a new industry keep reading, your audience line should name that industry. Those are instruction changes.

Your AI can't edit its own project instructions. You make the change:

1. **Approve the instruction changes** you want. The review labels each recommendation as a plan change or an instruction change.
2. **Make sure the AI has your exact instructions.** ChatGPT and Claude projects can read their own instructions word for word. Gemini Gems can't, so Gemini users open the Gem, click Edit, copy the text from the Instructions box, and paste it into the chat.
3. **Copy the full updated instructions** the AI gives you, and check the list of what changed.
4. **Replace your project's instructions.** Open your project's instructions, select everything, and paste the new version. In a Gem, edit the Gem and replace the text in the Instructions box. Save.
5. **Keep the old version.** Save a dated copy, like "Instructions 2026-11," before you replace them, so you can go back if a change doesn't work.

## Ground rules

- **You are the editor.** Read every draft before it goes anywhere. AI drafts, you decide.
- **Free plans have limits.** Free ChatGPT and Claude accounts have the tightest usage caps. Gemini's free tier is more generous. Plan your drafting sessions around them.
- **Publish it yourself.** Copy the approved post, start a post on LinkedIn, paste it, and check the formatting. Click the clock icon next to the Post button to schedule it for your day and time. It costs nothing.
