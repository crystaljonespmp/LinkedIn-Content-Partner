# Bonus: Network and Find Jobs on X

Companion to the [LinkedIn Content Partner project instructions](LinkedIn-Content-Partner-Project-Instructions.md) | By Crystal Jones

X has become a good place for professionals to network. It has no job board, but people post jobs there. You'll find good articles, people in your field, recruiters, and hiring managers. The purpose is the same as on LinkedIn: get seen by recruiters, hiring managers, and the peers who can connect you to hiring managers.

X is its own platform. It doesn't have to mirror LinkedIn. Your content, the days you post, and the times you post can all be different. You might post an idea on X one week and never post it on LinkedIn, or post it there later. The reverse works too.

## Why X is worth your time

- **Jobs get posted there.** Recruiters and hiring managers share openings on X.
- **The articles are good.** People in your field share and discuss articles and ideas worth reacting to. Add the best ones to your post inspiration list.
- **The people are reachable.** Professionals, recruiters, and executives post and reply on X, and a thoughtful reply can start a real conversation.

## Curate your feed

Garbage in, garbage out. If your feed is full of junk, it's because of what you follow, or because you follow almost nothing and the algorithm is guessing. Build your feed around your search:

- **Follow companies on three lists.** Companies in your industry, companies in adjacent industries, and companies you want to work for. Those aren't the same list.
- **Follow people who serve your search.** Recruiters, peers in your field, and executives who can connect you to hiring managers.
- **Join Communities in your field.** X Communities work like groups on LinkedIn.
- **Use Lists.** Group the accounts you care most about into a List, like "Recruiters" or "Target Companies," so you can check them without scrolling past everything else.
- **Tell X what you don't want.** On posts you don't want to see, tap the three dots and choose "Not interested in this post." Mute words for topics you want out of your feed.
- **Keep politics to a small slice.** Most of what you follow, like, and reply to should serve your search. If you engage mostly with politics, that's what your feed will show you.

What's worth following depends on your career, so build your own lists around your field and the roles you want.

Curating your feed is about what serves you. Networking is different. When you reach out to people, give as much as you take.

## Set up your profile

- **Name and photo:** use the same name and a similar professional photo as on LinkedIn, so people recognize you.
- **Bio:** say what you do and the kind of work you're focused on. Whether you mention that you're looking is up to you.
- **Link:** put your LinkedIn profile in the website field.
- **Pinned post:** pin one strong post that shows how you think.

## Find jobs on X

- **Search for openings.** Try searches like "hiring [your role]" or "[your role] hiring," then open the **Latest** tab to see the newest posts first.
- **Follow recruiters and company careers accounts** for the companies on your lists.
- **Check your Lists** every few days for new openings.

## Network on X

- **Reply before you reach out.** Thoughtful replies to someone's posts get you noticed. Add something useful, not just "Great post."
- **Share other people's work.** Repost articles from people in your field with a line about why it matters.
- **Send a direct message once you've interacted.** Keep it short and specific. Lead with what you have in common, not with a request.

## Post on X

You draft X posts the same way you draft LinkedIn posts: from your career stories, your resume, and your post inspiration list, with you as the editor.

**Free X accounts** limit each post to 280 characters. When an idea needs more room, write a short thread of 2 to 4 connected posts.

**X Premium accounts** can publish longer posts. Long posts still show only the first few lines in the feed, so the opening has to carry the point.

The scheduling tools from the main guide, Typefully, SocialBu, and AdaptlyPost, all post to X as well as LinkedIn.

## Measure X too

Add X to your monthly review. X's built-in analytics have changed several times in 2026, and free accounts see less than Premium accounts, so check what your account shows you. If your scheduling tool tracks X, pull the numbers from there. SocialBu tracks X as well as LinkedIn.

Judge X on its own results. It doesn't mirror LinkedIn, so a post that does well on one may not do well on the other.

## Add X to your project

Paste one of these add-ons at the end of your project instructions. Use the one that matches your X account. Fill in every [bracket].

### Free X account

```text
X POSTS
I also post on X. X is its own channel and does not mirror LinkedIn.
- Plan X separately: [number] posts per week on [days] at [time and time zone].
- Draft X posts from the same source files. Some ideas will go only to X, some only to LinkedIn, and some to both at different times.
- My X account is free, so each post must be 280 characters or fewer, including spaces, links, and hashtags. If an idea needs more room, write a thread of 2 to 4 numbered posts.
- When I say "plan X," propose a 4-week X calendar with the date, post type, source, and the opening line. Wait for my approval.
- When I say "adapt [post] for X," rewrite that LinkedIn post so it fits X. Do not copy it word for word.
- Use 1 or 2 hashtags at most.
- The same rules apply as on LinkedIn: keep employers anonymous, follow my choice about mentioning my job search, and nothing goes out without my approval.
- When I give you my X numbers with my monthly review, review X separately from LinkedIn and recommend up to 3 changes to my X plan.
- [Only if you connect a scheduling tool] When I say "schedule X," show me the approved X posts with dates and times, then schedule only those posts to X in [tool name].
```

## Starter prompts for X

- "Plan X for the next 4 weeks."
- "Give me 5 X post ideas from my career stories that I haven't used on LinkedIn."
- "Adapt my approved LinkedIn post about [topic] for X."
- "Read this link from my inspiration list and draft a short X post reacting to it."
